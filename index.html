Moment 1 · The product page

He loves the album. He wonders: "Will it reach before Tuesday?"

Your product pages say it clearly: priority orders reach metros in 4 working days and the rest of India in 5, standard orders in 6 and 7, counted from the next day and excluding Sundays and holidays. That is accurate. But Karan has to do the calendar sums himself, at 9 pm, with an anniversary at stake.

On Loving Crafts today (public pages)
  • Delivery shown as working-day ranges: priority 4 / 5 days, standard 6 / 7 days.
  • Counts exclude Sundays and public holidays, so the shopper works out the date.
  • We did not find a pincode check on the product page.
On Fastrr
  • Dynamic EDD: a real date for his pincode, for each delivery option.
  • Built from the network that ships the parcel, using live courier performance, not a static rule.
  • Repeated at checkout, so the promise on the page is the promise at payment.

Moment 2 · The cart

Priority shipping is already your best add-on. In the cart, it can show the date it buys.

Your site offers one upsell: "Speed up your delivery with priority shipping". The cart drawer then says "shipping calculated at checkout". For a shopper counting days, the date is what sells priority, and a gift pairing is what lifts the basket.

On Loving Crafts today (public pages)
  • Priority shipping is offered as an upgrade on product pages.
  • Cart note: "Taxes included. Discounts and shipping calculated at checkout."
  • Free shipping at ₹1,000+ is a site banner, not visible in the cart as it happens.
On Fastrr
  • Delivery options with dates: "Priority, Mon 30 Nov" next to "Standard, Wed 2 Dec", so the upgrade sells itself.
  • Milestone bar on your own rule: free shipping at ₹1,000 shows as unlocked, with a next step you choose.
  • Expandable AI upsell: a matching bouquet or frame opens inside the cart and is added in one tap.

Moment 3 · The checkout

One page. Nothing to type. He is done in a few taps, and back to planning the surprise.

Karan has ordered gifts before, and he is ordering on a phone with a deadline. Every field he types is time he does not have, and a mistyped pincode on a made-to-order gift is a problem you pay for.

On Loving Crafts today (public pages)
  • Standard store checkout: shipping and discounts are calculated once the shopper reaches it.
  • A new shopper types name, address, pincode and phone on a phone.
  • Photos are uploaded after ordering, through an SMS link or the "Send Photos" page.
On Fastrr
  • Address pre-fill above 95%, from addresses actually delivered across the Shiprocket network.
  • The same delivery date at checkout that he picked in the cart.
  • SSO: returning shoppers skip OTPs, address entry and payment selection.
  • One page, in your look, so it still feels like Loving Crafts.

Moment 4 · Payment and risk

He pays in one tap. Behind the scenes, every order is read before it is made.

Most shoppers want to pay and move on. But a personalised album that comes back cannot be resold, so one returned order costs you the shipping and the product. Your site offers partial COD to protect against exactly this. The network can tell which orders are likely to come back before you start making them.

On Loving Crafts today (public pages)
  • Partial COD: an advance up front, the rest on delivery.
  • Two pages word it differently: "50% upfront" on one, "₹500 in advance" on another.
  • Prepaid orders get the same flow as everyone else.
On Fastrr
  • QuickPay: one-tap UPI through his preferred app, pre-selected.
  • Payment personalisation: each shopper sees the method and offer they are most likely to pay with, turning COD intent into prepaid.
  • RTO risk tags from pincode, address and buyer history, scored against real delivery outcomes before the order is made.
  • Pay After Delivery: a hesitant shopper approves with UPI and is charged only after delivery. Live on Coitonic.
  • 0% MDR on prepaid with eligible gateways. Commercials by payment mode are shared separately.
